This textbook aims to provide a systematic introduction to the mathematical and computational principles of deep representation learning to senior undergraduate and beginning graduate students. The main prerequisite for this book is undergraduate linear algebra, probability and statistics, and optimization. Some familiarity with basic concepts from information theory, feedback control, and signal processing would enhance your appreciation. 

This book studies a common and fundamental problem that exists behind almost all modern machine learning practices. The problem is how to effectively and efficiently learn a low-dimensional distribution of data in a high-dimensional space and then transform the distribution to a compact and structured representation. This representation can generally be referred to as {\em memory} or {\em knowledge} learned from the sensed data. 

We will start with the most basic and classical models of Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Independent Component Analysis (ICA), and Dictionary Learning (DL) that assume the distribution to have low-dimensional linear and independent structures. Through these simple idealistic models, we will introduce the basic concepts and algorithmic ideas of how to learn a low-dimensional distribution.

To generalize these classical models and their solutions to general data distributions, we introduce a universal computational principle for learning low-dimensional distributions: {\em compression}. As we will see, data compression provides a unifying view on all past and present approaches for distribution or representation learning such as Score Matching (for denoising) and coding Rate Reduction (for lossy compression). Within this framework, all modern Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), such as ResNet and Transformer, can all be mathematically interpreted as (unrolled) optimization algorithms to iteratively achieve better compression and representation in terms of reducing entropy and gaining information. 

Furthermore, to ensure that the learned representation is correct and consistent, we will study the effective {\em auto-encoding} architectures that consist of both encoding and decoding (say for denoising and diffusion). In order for a learning system to be fully automatic and continuous, we will also study a powerful {\em closed-loop transcription framework} that enables encoding and decoding networks to self-correct and thus self-improve, via the ubiquitous closed-loop feedback mechanism. 

To connect theory to practice, we will demonstrate many applications of the methods and algorithms developed in this book for real-world tasks with large scale data such as image classification, image completion, image segmentation, image generation, as well as similar tasks for text data.
