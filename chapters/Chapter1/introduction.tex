\section{Intelligence}
The notion of intelligence can be very  general and broad. But that often makes it ambiguous. Here, we here try to give a more precise description about what it mainly is. Our definition may have significantly narrowed the scope, but make it more implementable, verifiable, and even measurable (via computable means). 

\subsection{Pursuit of Low-dimensionality}
A main purpose, and a fundamental task,  of any intelligent system is to learn what is predictable in its environment, memorize it, and use it to make better decisions for better reward. 

Data and observations that carry predictable information manifest in many different forms. For example, they can be sequences that are predictable (Andrey Kolmogorov, Claude Shannon, later Ray Solomonoff etc...), or  continuous states that are subject to  dynamical models (differential equations). But a unifying characteristic of such data is that their distributions are always have very low intrinsic dimensions, hence highly compressible. 

Many (largely equivalent) ways to measure compactness of data: dimension and volume of (the support of) a distribution, Kolmogorov complexity, minimum description length, rate distortion etc. 

\subsection{A Computational Approach}
How to achieve the goal through efficient computation? The most effective way to learn is through certain form of ``compression'' (which we will make precise later.)   It is crucial to differentiate computability (universal machines), tractability (P versus NP), and practicality (minimal optimization cost, scalable)... The last is actually very important for understanding  intelligence, at least for what have taken place in nature.
\begin{equation}
    \mbox{\textbf{Computable}} \;
   \Longrightarrow \; \mbox{\textbf{Tractable}} \; \Longrightarrow \; 
   \mbox{\textbf{Scalable}}.
\end{equation}
Low-level intelligent functions (e.g. memory forming) deal with massive high-dimensional sensory data, hence must be {\em scalable}: operations that rely only on first-order oracles (subspaces, gradients) or  second-order statistics (covariance and correlation)... Computational complexity is linear (or even sublinear) in the dimension and number of samples.

Optimization strategies that are more {\em natural} to realize with less physical requirements, amenable to local, parallel and distributed implementations. 

\subsection{A System Perspective}
Clarify the relationship and difference between memory (learned  information) and intelligence. The basic characteristics of an intelligent system: the ability to memorize existing information, improve it, and acquire new one, preferably autonomously. What are the necessary mechanisms for a system to achieve this? One-directional open-ended, bi-directional auto-encoding, and closed-loop systems:  
\begin{equation}
    \mbox{\textbf{Open-ended}} \; \Longrightarrow \; 
    \mbox{\textbf{Bi-directional}} \;  \Longrightarrow \; \mbox{\textbf{Closed-loop}}.
\end{equation}
Autoencoding and autonomous  error correction via  {\em closed-loop transcription}.


\section{The History}

\subsection{Computation, Cybernetics, and Artificial Intelligence}
The magical eras of theoretical formulation for computability, cybernetics, and artificial intelligence: The 40's (computer,  cybernetics) and the 50's (artificial intelligence). 

Inspiration from Science. A biography of Norbert Wiener: ``Dark Hero of the Information Age: In Search Of Norbert Wiener--Father of Cybernetics'' by Conway and Siegelman. Connections to many biologists. Wiener studied zoology as a student.

Within the realm of biology, Lorenz and Tinbergen, who were pioneers in studying animal behavior (though not intelligence per se). In cognitive science, there is a long history, including behaviorists (reinforcement learning), Gestalt psychologists etc. 

How to conduct computation by machines (the von Neumann architecture and the Turing machine). How to encode and decode useful information (Information Theory by Shannon).  And how to correct errors in prediction via closed-loop feedback (Wiener) and optimize decision-making via game (von Neumann). 



\subsection{PCA, Autoencoding, and ICA}
In terms of {\em learning a low-dimensional distribution} and exploiting it, let us start with the simplest but arguably the most important special case, that has a long history: the classic problem of learning a low-dimensional distribution modeled by a linear subspace (PCA in Chapter \ref{ch:classic}), dimension-reduction via denoising, and consistent representation via autoencoding (proejcting and lifting). This idealistic model makes it easy and intuitive to convey some of the most fundamental ideas for  learning a low-dim distribution, including the important notion of compression and denoising... 

Independent component analysis (ICA)  \cite{Ans-1985} as a classic model for {\em learning a good representation} (in fact originally proposed as a simple model for our memory). Later theoretic and algorithmic developments by Erkki Oja and Aapo Hyv\"{a}rinen etc. (refer to Chapter \ref{ch:classic}). 




\subsection{Compression via Denoising and Diffusion}
Parallel developments in statistical learning and machine learning: How to effectively and efficiently learn a more general low-dimensional distribution in a high-dimensional space? The history of denoising, empirical Bayesian denoising in 1950's and 1960's, Tweedie's formula, image denoising in 1990's, score function in early 2000's, and later studies... Refer technical details to Chapter \ref{ch:compression}.

\subsection{Artificial Neural Networks}

{\em Inspiration from Neuroscience and Cognition.}
Inception of artificial neural networks in 1943, by the work of Pitts and McCullough. Warren McCulloch was a psychiatry professor at the University of Chicago. Within neuroscience, certainly the work of Hubel and Wiesel on visual cortex, as well as David Van Essen and his famous wiring diagram of neocortex, inspired convnets.

On the computational side, perceptron and early forms of artificial neural networks in 1960's, problem with XOR in 1969 (later resolved with deeper architectures), and persistent empirical efforts in practicing deeper neural network architecture from the 1980's till the 2010's (LeNet etc), despite two winters in 70's (due to XOR) and 2000's (due to SVM)... This is also the time when back propagation (BP) was (re)invented and practiced. 

Connection to learning low-dimensional distributions or representations: early roles of neural networks for PCA \cite{Oja1982SimplifiedNM,Baldi89},  Nonlinear PCA \cite{Kramer1991NonlinearPC}, and ICA.  Autoencoding with multiple-layer architectures by Hinton et. al. in 1980's and 1990's...  and later work \cite{Hinton504} and survey by Baldi \cite{Baldi2011}. (refer to Chapter \ref{ch:consistent}).




 
\section{The Modern Era}

\subsection{Past Two Decades}


\subsubsection{The Practice of Deep Neural Networks}
The deep learning revolution started 2010's: AlexNet \cite{Krizhevsky2012imagenet}, ResNet, Transformers etc, and its empirical successes but lack of theoretical explanation (refer to Chapter \ref{ch:representation}). Their early successes in supervised learning and recognition (encoding). 

Later rise of generative methods (decoding). Learning data distribution via auto-encoding, learning a generative model (a decoder) via discriminative methods such as Tu's work and GAN, autoregressive models, and denoising and diffusion (refer to Chapter \ref{ch:compression}). Many variants of the principle of compression, often in disguise. 

\subsubsection{The Theory of High-Dimensional  Data Analysis} The compressive sensing revolution started in 2000's, and its theoretical successes but limitations in practice (refer to Chapter \ref{ch:classic}) \cite{Wright-Ma-2022}. From PCA, ICA, to sparse models, and more general low-dimensional models... providing strong correctness guarantee and computational efficiency for those idealistic models.  

\subsection{The Present}
\subsubsection{Understanding Artificial Neural Networks}
Can we have a theoretical framework for understanding artificial  neural networks? How the above two branches of knowledge are connected? In particular, how does the theory of high-dim data analysis provide theoretical justification to the practice of deep neural networks? 

From the perspective of a unifying principle: {\em maximizing information gain}, to formulate a new mathematical framework and computational objective for distribution learning and representation learning. This framework connects high-dimensional data analysis and deep neural networks. In this framework, the role of deep networks can be precisely interpreted as to emulate iterative optimization algorithms in an unrolled fashion, for information gain (refer to Chapter \ref{ch:representation}) 

\subsubsection{Learning Consistent Representations}
To ensure the learned internal representation is {\em consistent} with the sensed data distribution (refer to Chapter \ref{ch:autoencoding}) and the learning can be done internally and {\em autonomously} (refer to Chapter \ref{ch:self-consistent}). This entails integrating ideas from closed-loop feedback and game.

\subsubsection{Bridging Theory and Practice}
The challenge of bridging the gap between theory and practice, Chapter \ref{ch:applications}... the importance of bridging theory and practice from what is computable, to what is tractable, and to what is parsimonious and scalable. The last category of computing is the most important to the study of intelligence, at least that occurring in nature. Mathematically, we need to sort out which classes of low-dimensional distributions are easy to learn, with what universal ``efficient'' operations or processes. 

\subsection{The Future}
\subsubsection{Towards General Intelligence}
High-level functions such as causal inference, logical inference, and abstract and symbolic computation, which were meant to be studied in the program of ``artificial intelligence.'' Is compression enough?

The difference between learning and applying such knowledge via external supervision and developing and improving such knowledge autonomously. 

How to systematically and objectively evaluate the level of knowledge and intelligence for any given system? Refined definition of Turing test seems needed.

\subsubsection{Towards Natural Intelligence}
Discover new computational mechanisms that have enabled the realization of scalable operations and architectures under natural physical conditions and constraints, say without the option of global optimization (without resorting to BP \cite{Back-Prop}).

Optimization strategies that are more {\em natural} to realize with less physical requirements, amenable to local, parallel and distributed implementations:
\begin{equation}
   \mbox{\textbf{Scalable}} \; \Longrightarrow \; 
   \mbox{\textbf{Natural}}.
\end{equation}
Say, forward optimization and local closed-loop error correction?
